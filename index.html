Lucas Y. Tian, Ph.D.

I study how operations in the brain give rise to intelligent behavior. I am interested in operations implemented in the activity of circuits of neurons in the brain, and in the kind of intelligence that allows animals and humans to solve new problems through internal processes, such as reasoning and inference. I approach this by recording spiking activity of large (100s) of neurons in primates performing difficult cognitive tasks. I use a variety of techniques to explain the data, including building mechanistic computational models (e.g., neural network models). My current focus is on structured cognitive processes involved in constructing and executing hierachically structured actions to solve new physical problems. I came up with a way train monkeys on a drawing-like task in which they make novel compositional drawings. In two foundational findings, we discovered that trained monkeys have an internal representation of symbolically structured actions (e.g., the abstract concept of a "circle" stroke), and that this representation corresponds to specific, localized, area of frontal cortex called ventral premotor cortex.
