Lucas Y. Tian, Ph.D.

I study how operations in the brain give rise to intelligent behavior. I am interested in operations implemented in the activity of neurons, and in the kind of intelligence that allows animals and humans to solve new problems through internal processes—such as reasoning, inference, and imagination—rather than being told what to do through external guidance.

I approach this by recording the spiking activity of many (hundreds of) neurons in monkeys performing difficult cognitive tasks. I use a variety of techniques to analyze these data, including biologically inspired computational models (e.g., neural network models).

My current focus is on how the brain constructs and executes new hierarchically structured actions to solve novel physical problems. I developed a drawing-like task in which monkeys produce novel compositional drawings of geometric figures resembling human handwriting.

In a foundational study, I discovered that trained monkeys possess an internal representation of symbolically structured actions (e.g., the abstract concept of a circle stroke), and that this representation corresponds to a specific, localized area of frontal cortex: ventral premotor cortex (Tian et al., Nature, accepted in principle; preprint here). These “action symbols” may correspond to abstract action concepts that allow us to plan and organize complex action sequences (e.g., making dinner), play music, learn sports, and otherwise interact intelligently with the physical world.
